SIGs and LCGs

Two ways to work with your peers. Special Interest Groups share what we should know. The new Leadership Challenge Groups decide what we should do.

What should we know?

Special Interest Groups

Monthly online sessions where members work through the topics shaping customer education, led by volunteer topic leads.

Who attends
Practitioners and members interested in the topic, with topic leads shaping each area
Format
A 60-minute topic-centered webinar, once a month, organized by a topic council
Goal
Knowledge sharing and insight, with value all year between the two conferences
How SIGs work

What should we do? New

Leadership Challenge Groups

A working session where leaders solve one real challenge together. It adds to the SIGs rather than replacing them.

Who attends
Leaders and decision-makers, but open to all members, with no job title required
Format
A 60-minute working session under the Chatham House Rule, where everyone contributes. Not a webinar.
Goal
Solve one real challenge and leave with one insight, one connection, and one action
How LCGs work

What should we know?

Special Interest Groups

One 60-minute online session a month at 3pm UK (10am Eastern, 7am Pacific), rotating through four topics with volunteer leads.

  • Every session opens with “what are we trying to solve?”, and members often share real documents.
  • Sessions are open to all members. Topic leads confirm each session's focus.
  • Records of earlier meetings stay in the members area.

What should we do? New

Leadership Challenge Groups

A 60-minute working session where members solve one real challenge together. A summary of themes, commitments, and future topics follows within 48 hours.

Launching soon. Dates are to be confirmed, and “Leadership Challenge Group” is a working name.

One meeting, seven steps

  1. 01Welcome and ground rules5 min
  2. 02Roundtable pulse check5 min
  3. 03Wins and challenges roundtable10 min
  4. 04Challenge selection5 min
  5. 05Challenge scrum20 min
  6. 06Commitment round10 min
  7. 07Closing5 min

Six principles

Confidentiality

The Chatham House Rule applies.

Participation

Everyone contributes.

Experience before theory

Successes, failures, and lessons learned count equally.

No solicitation

Speak as a practitioner: no demos, marketing, or pitches.

Mutual advocacy

Members help each other succeed.

Accountability

Every meeting ends with an action, reviewed at the next one.

What you gain

  • New insights and practical solutions to current challenges
  • Greater confidence in decision-making
  • Exposure to different perspectives
  • Clear next actions

How we know it works

“What changed because we met?”

Every participant finishes the sentence “Before our next meeting I will…” with a commitment that is specific, observable, achievable, and personal.
