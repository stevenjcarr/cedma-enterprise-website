SIGs and LCGs

Two ways to learn from your peers. Special Interest Groups share what we should know. The new Leadership Challenge Groups decide what we should do.

What should we know?

Special Interest Groups

Monthly online sessions where members learn about the topics shaping customer education, led by volunteer presenters.

Who attends
Members and non-members interested in learning more about a topic.
Format
A 60-minute topic-centered webinar presented by a peer.
Goal
Stay current on the topics that matter, and hear how your peers are handling them.
How SIGs work

What should we do? New

Leadership Challenge Groups

An ongoing group where leaders solve real challenges together.

Who attends
Members only. Leaders and others who shape decisions, whatever their title. Limited to 12 people so the group can build trust.
Format
A 60-minute working session under the Chatham House Rule, where everyone contributes. Not a webinar.
Goal
Solve one real challenge and leave with one insight, one connection, and one action.
How LCGs work

What should we know?

Special Interest Groups

Monthly at 4pm UK (5pm Central Europe, 11am Eastern, 8am Pacific). Every session opens with “what are we trying to solve?”, and members often share real use cases.

View the SIG calendar

Four topics

Topic 1

Future-proofing the education organization

How to structure teams and roles for the AI era, and what to build, buy, or retire as the work changes.

Topic 2

ROI measurement

Methods for measuring the return on education programs and making the case to finance and executive leaders.

Topic 3

Skills gap and reskilling

How the skills your teams need are changing as AI reshapes roles, and how to close the gap.

Topic 4

STOP-START-CONTINUE

A candid look at what still works without AI, what to stop, and what to start.

Present at a SIG

We’re looking for members to present a solution they’ve built or a result they’ve delivered. You lead a 60-minute session: walk us through what you did, what worked, and what you’d change, then take questions from your peers. How often each topic runs depends on who volunteers.

Volunteer to present
  • Visibility with senior education leaders across the membership
  • Candid peer feedback on your approach, before you scale it
  • A platform to share your team’s work and establish your thought leadership
  • A direct hand in shaping what the community learns next

What should we do? New

Leadership Challenge Groups

Members bring the challenges and choose the subjects, including topics conferences rarely have time to cover. Sessions run at 4pm UK (5pm Central Europe, 11am Eastern, 8am Pacific), and a summary of themes, commitments, and future topics follows within 48 hours.

Six principles

Confidentiality

The Chatham House Rule applies.

Participation

Everyone contributes.

Experience before theory

Successes, failures, and lessons learned count equally.

No solicitation

Speak as a practitioner: no demos, marketing, or pitches.

Mutual advocacy

Members help each other succeed.

Accountability

Every meeting ends with an action, reviewed at the next one.

What you gain

  • Peer advice from leaders at comparable enterprise organizations
  • A trusted setting to test decisions before you take them to your executives
  • A direct say in which challenges and subjects the group takes on
  • Benchmarks and approaches you can apply the same week
  • A standing network of leaders who support you beyond the session

How we know it works

“What changed because we met?”

Every participant finishes the sentence “Before our next meeting I will…” with a commitment that is specific, observable, achievable, and personal.

One meeting, seven steps

  1. 5 min
    Welcome and ground rulesWe open the session and confirm the ground rules, including the Chatham House Rule.
  2. 5 min
    Roundtable pulse checkEveryone says briefly how things are going for them right now.
  3. 10 min
    Wins and challenges roundtableEach member shares a recent win and a current challenge.
  4. 5 min
    Challenge selectionThe group picks one challenge to work on together.
  5. 20 min
    Challenge scrumThe group works through the chosen challenge, drawing on what has worked for them.
  6. 10 min
    Commitment roundEach member commits to one specific action to take before the next meeting.
  7. 5 min
    ClosingA short recap, and ideas for future topics.
